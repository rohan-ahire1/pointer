Sliding Window

Look out of a train window. New scenery slides in on the right, old scenery slides out on the left, and you never re-read the whole view. You only deal with what changed.

⏱ about 15 minutes·Needs: arrays, sets·Time O(n)

Reach for it when…

  • ✓ the answer is a contiguous run
  • ✓ it asks for the longest, shortest or a count
  • ✓ brute force is two nested loops

Sliding Window in 60 seconds [EXPLAINER VIDEO] · 1:00
No sound? Captions are on by default.

0:00 — Say you need the longest stretch of a string with no repeated letters. The obvious way checks every start and every end. That’s n² work.

0:15 — Instead, keep a window. Move its right edge one letter at a time and remember what’s inside.

0:32 — When a letter repeats, slide the left edge forward until the repeat is gone. The window never moves backwards.

0:48 — Each letter enters once and leaves once. One pass, O(n). That’s the sliding window.
